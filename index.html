THE PRICE OF BLOOD: EXPOSING THE COLONIAL BLUFF AND THE MYTH OF UNEARNED PEACE

By Yogesh Pokharel

This essay dismantles the myth of a purely peaceful British exit, arguing that India's freedom was won through armed rebellion, economic sabotage resistance and military mutiny rather than polite petitions. Tracing a century of defiance from the industrial foundries of Lahore and the Jallianwala Bagh massacre to the Ghadar underground, Bhagat Singh's gallows and the 1946 Royal Indian Navy mutiny, it exposes how the British Empire only retreated once native soldiers turned their guns against the Raj.
