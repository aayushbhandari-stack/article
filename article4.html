The Tiger’s Next Leap: How Rabindra Dhant’s Heartbreaking Story and Unmatched Hype Can Create Nepal’s First UFC Star

By Yogesh Pokharel

October 2, 2026

Go to any local tea stall, open up TikTok or check Instagram right now: the entire Nepali sports world is completely gripped by Rabindra Dhant fever. Affectionately known to all of us as "Bajhang ko Bagh" (The Tiger of Bajhang), Dhant has taken the international mixed martial arts world by storm. Fresh off a nail-biting, heart-stopping split-decision victory over New Zealand’s undefeated powerhouse Kasib Murdoch in the Road to UFC semifinals in Shanghai, he stands just one win away from securing a historic, guaranteed UFC contract.

When you look at where he came from, this isn't just an athletic run. It is a movie script brought to life.

Out of the Darkness: A Story of Grit and Humility

To understand why every Nepali heart beats faster when Rabindra steps into the cage, you have to look at his roots. Born in the remote, impoverished district of Bajhang, his early life was defined by survival. To escape crushing poverty, he had to leave home as a young boy of 16, migrating to India to work grueling manual labor jobs like breaking stones, loading trucks and carrying heavy electric poles. In a recent interview, he openly shared how the back-breaking toil left his body broken, earning barely Rs. 400 per day. Later in New Delhi, he was cleaning offices and serving tea just to stay afloat. As he slept in stone-walled rooms riddled with holes, he dreamt of becoming a global cricket superstar for Nepal.

It was during these darkest hours, waking up in the freezing pre-dawn dark to train in karate before his shifts, that he found his way to martial arts. He realized his fists were his only ticket out of a broken cycle.

When he finally returned to Nepal, he poured his sweat into our local circuits, training under Lock N Roll MMA in Kathmandu and later splitting time with Soma Fight Club in Bali. Then came August 2025 at Matrix Fight Night (MFN) 17, where he knocked out India's Chungreng Koren to become the first-ever Nepali fighter to win a major professional MMA title.

Yet, despite his meteoric rise, what hits home for us is his profound humility. He carries zero arrogance. He treats every opponent with immense respect and speaks with the gentle, soft-spoken politeness of a guy who has never forgotten the tea stalls of Delhi or the dust of Bajhang. He fights carrying the heavy weight, the hopes and the dreams of an entire nation on his shoulders, yet remains completely grounded.

The Road to UFC War: Surviving the Semifinal Grinder

To understand how hard this journey is, look at what he just pulled off in the Road to UFC Season 5. After destroying Kimbert Alintozon with a violent TKO in the quarterfinals, the semifinal match against New Zealand’s Kasib Murdoch was an absolute war.

In a major tournament like the Road to UFC, winning a semifinal is everything: it puts you just one victory away from a guaranteed contract with the world's premier fighting organization, the UFC. Murdoch wasn't just any fighter; he came out of a notoriously well-established global gym system with elite wrestling and grappling pedigree.

The fight was a brutal, grueling chess match. It wasn't an easy walk in the park; it was a back-andforth war where every single second felt like an hour. When the judges finally announced a split decision (two judges scored it for Dhant and one for Murdoch), every Nepali watching across the globe collectively held their breath before erupting in celebration. He survived the fire, weathered the grappling storms of an elite prospect, and gutted out a win through sheer, unbreakable Nepali willpower.

The Corporate Hype: UFC is Already Watching

The UFC is a massive global business, and trust me, they know exactly what they have in Rabindra. If you look at the official UFC Instagram and Facebook pages, they have been spamming clips of Dhant because his content drives insane digital engagement.

The UFC’s media team tracks the metrics. They see the hundreds of thousands of views, the endless walls of Nepali flags in the comments, and the massive spikes in viewership whenever his face hits the screen. In MMA, fighters spend a decade trying to build a loyal fanbase; Dhant walked in with an entire nation already riding behind him. This digital leverage gives him massive corporate backing. The UFC knows that signing Dhant instantly unlocks an entirely new, untapped South Asian market. His humble background paired with this explosive digital hype makes him a promoter’s absolute dream.

The Technical Bottleneck: Leveling Up for the Final Stage

As he prepares for the tournament finals, however, we have to look at the cold, hard technical realities of the sport. While Dhant has the iron chin, raw power and warrior spirit to beat anyone, his striking style has sometimes leaned heavily on his boxing foundations. To dominate elite global competition in the UFC, a fighter cannot be one-dimensional. He needs to sharpen a few key elements:

  1. Muay Thai Elements (The Art of Eight Limbs): Using elbows and knees: devastating weapons in the close-quarters battle against the cage fence.
  2. Kick-to-Punch Setups: Adding diverse low kicks to chop down an opponent's legs. Kicks slow down movement and drop the enemy's guard so punches land cleaner.
  3. Unorthodox Striking:Blending in unpredictable movements like spinning backfists or dynamic kicks so high-level opponents can't easily read his rhythm.

The UFC’s 135-pound bantamweight division is a literal shark tank filled with world-class tacticians who study tape for months to solve a fighter's style. Continuing to sharpen these weapons at Soma Fight Club for the finals is crucial to keeping his opponent guessing every second of the fight.

The Future Move: From Regional Prospect to Global Contender

Right now, Dhant’s coaches are doing an incredible job building him up, but winning that UFC contract will change everything.

Once he signs that big-league contract, transitioning to legendary international mega-gyms like City Kickboxing in New Zealand , American Top Team or Kill Cliff FC in Florida, or the American Kickboxing Academy (AKA) in California will become vital. To break into the top 15 bantamweights on earth, he will need specialized sparring partners, elite wrestling coaches and advanced sports science to elevate his fight IQ —his tactical decision-making and awareness inside the chaos of the cage.

The Tiger’s Destiny: A Star in the Making

Rabindra Dhant has the heart of a champion, the backing of a passionate nation, and the soul of a true Nepali warrior. He went from washing dishes and surviving on Rs. 400 a day to trading blows on a global stage in Shanghai.

The raw power and grit are already there to conquer the final hurdle of the Road to UFC tournament. If he pairs his natural humility and our unmatched crowd hype with a continuous technical evolution at elite camps, the "Tiger of Bajhang" won't just make it to the UFC, but will become a global superstar, flying our red and blue flag all the way to the top.
